The rating math

Modeled on tennis Elo (the FiveThirtyEight approach), adapted for doubles.

Expected result

EA = 1 / (1 + 10(RB − RA)/400) — a 100-point edge ≈ 64% win probability. Everyone starts at 1500.

Dynamic K-factor

K = 250 / (matches + 5)0.4. New players move ~130 pts/match; veterans settle near 30. Tennis Elo's signature feature — ratings converge fast, then stabilize.

Doubles

Team rating is the average of both players. Partners share one expected result but each updates with their own K, so a newcomer swings more than the veteran they're paired with.

Margin of victory

m = 0.75 + 0.5 × (pointDiff / winnerScore), clamped to 0.75–1.25. An 11–9 grind moves ~0.79×; an 11–0 shutout 1.25×.

Serving first

Recorded on every match and analyzed in Serve Analysis, but deliberately excluded from the rating formula. Testing whether it matters requires the ratings stay independent of it — if you adjusted for serve while measuring it, you'd be measuring your own assumption.

Storage & access

Everything lives in pickleball.db (SQLite) next to the server. The page polls every 5s, so matches entered by others appear automatically. Ratings are always recomputed from the full match log, so deleting a bad entry cleanly rewrites history.

Two PINs. The friend PIN lets anyone add players and record matches. The admin PIN is additionally required to delete anything or change settings. Both are stored only as scrypt hashes, and wrong guesses trigger exponential lockout — five misses, then doubling delays up to an hour.

Closing the tunnel or stopping the server never loses data — every match is written to disk immediately.
